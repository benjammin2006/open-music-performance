# Artist, Venue and Organizer Friction in US Live Music Ticketing, Booking, Payment, Data and Promotion (2024–2026)

Scope note: the focus is the US. UK and Australian figures appear only as labelled comparators. Research was done 2026-10-14. Evidence-strength tags used below:
- **[SURVEY]**: a structured survey with a stated sample.
- **[TRADE]**: trade-press reporting or an organization's position statement.
- **[VENDOR]**: vendor marketing or a competitor's comparison page; biased by design.
- **[ANECDOTE]**: a first-person account or a single reported incident.

Segment codes:
- **(a)** touring or signed artists at large venues
- **(b)** independent artists at clubs and bars
- **(c)** small venue operators
- **(d)** volunteer or educational organizers

---

## 1. Economics of small independent venues (NIVA data; UK Music Venue Trust as comparator)

### Takeaway
Most US independent stages lose money. NIVA's first State of Live study found 64% unprofitable in 2024, and its June 2026 Live Pulse found 56% of operators with advance ticket sales down year over year. Rising costs, especially artist guarantees, staff and insurance, are the main pressures, not ticketing fees as such. No public US source gives the share of a small venue's revenue lost to ticketing fees, or the share of small shows ticketed in advance versus sold for cash at the door. The UK comparator is similarly thin-margin: 0.5% sector margin in 2023, and 53.8% of venues made no profit in 2025.

### Cited Findings
**US: NIVA State of Live (data year 2024, released June 2025) [SURVEY/TRADE]**
- 64% of independent stages nationwide were unprofitable in 2024. State rates: FL 65%, CA 69%, OH 80%, NY 81% — [Digital Music News, Oct 2025](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2025/10/10/niva-study-hidden-economic-power-independent-venues/)
- Total economic output is put at $153.1 billion, with $86.2 billion direct contribution to US GDP, 907,000+ jobs and $51.7B+ in wages — [Hypebot, Jan 2026](https://www.hypebot.com/why-ticket-sales-arent-enough-for-independent-music-venues-anymore). DMN printed "$153.1 million" and "$86.2 million", which looks like a typo given its own URL slug — [DMN](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2025/10/10/niva-study-hidden-economic-power-independent-venues/)
- Costs operators expected to rise: artist and booking fees (60%), employees (58%), insurance (55%), alcohol (48%), rent (45%) — [Hypebot summary of NIVA State of Live, via search snippet](https://www.hypebot.com/why-ticket-sales-arent-enough-for-independent-music-venues-anymore.md)
- Hypebot and TSE Entertainment argue that ticket revenue has become "a customer-acquisition tool… important, but insufficient on their own". Bar sales, venues' second-largest revenue source, are reported to be declining — [Hypebot, Jan 2026](https://www.hypebot.com/why-ticket-sales-arent-enough-for-independent-music-venues-anymore)
- NIVA's ED reportedly said 80% of venues reported ticket sales at their lowest since the 2008 recession, and argued the FTC all-in pricing rule is confusing consumers (2025, secondary snippet; unverified primary) — [search summary of NIVA/Hypebot coverage](https://www.hypebot.com/niva-bandsintown-survey-tracks-state-of-indie-live-events.md)

**US: NIVA × Bandsintown "Live Pulse" (first monthly edition, June 2026; 212+ independent venues, promoters and festivals) [SURVEY]**
- Advance sales year over year: 56% down (10% significantly, 45% slightly), 20% stable, 24% up — [Hypebot, Jun 17 2026](https://www.hypebot.com/niva-bandsintown-survey-tracks-state-of-indie-live-events.md)
- Walk-up sales: 65% average, 15% stronger than usual, 20% much weaker — [same](https://www.hypebot.com/niva-bandsintown-survey-tracks-state-of-indie-live-events.md)
- Top bottom-line pressure:

  | Pressure | Share |
  |---|---|
  | Ticket sales/attendance | 23% |
  | Artist fees/guarantees | 14% |
  | Staffing | 10% |
  | Inflation/consumer spending | 9% |
  | Rising costs (general) | 9% |
  | Bar sales | 8% |
  | Insurance | 7% |
  | Multinational competition | 4% |
  | **Marketing/awareness** | **4%** |

  Source: [same](https://www.hypebot.com/niva-bandsintown-survey-tracks-state-of-indie-live-events.md)
- Six-month viability score averaged 6.8/10. 12% scored low (1–4). Operators say "they can only increase ticket prices so far before hitting a consumer ceiling" — [same](https://www.hypebot.com/niva-bandsintown-survey-tracks-state-of-indie-live-events.md)

**US: closures and local texture [TRADE/ANECDOTE]**
- Pollstar (Dec 2025) reports narrowing margins from fuel, lodging and food costs plus consolidation. It notes The Mayan (LA, 1,500 cap) shuttering — [Pollstar, Dec 15 2025](https://news.pollstar.com/2025/12/15/you-know-it-aint-easy-the-mixed-bag-of-independent-live-businesses)
- Twin Cities: Palmer's Bar closed in September 2025 after 119 years. Smaller operators say "only a few hundred dollars remain" after payroll and bills. Taxes, service charges and artist splits take a large but unquantified share of gross. The Minneapolis Music Census drew 2,200+ responses — [Hoodline, Feb 2026](https://hoodline.com/2026/02/twin-cities-music-clubs-on-the-brink-as-costs-crush-the-scene/)
- No national US count of independent venue closures since 2020 was found.

**Ticketing fees and the venue's share [TRADE]**
- Ticketmaster's own FAQ, as quoted in 2021, says service fees vary "based on our agreement with each individual client" and are "typically shared with our clients", meaning venues and promoters get rebates. New York legal analysts argued such rebates may violate NY law — [TicketNews, 2021](https://www.ticketnews.com/2021/04/illegal-venue-rebates-new-york-ticketing-hearing/,)
- The fee-rebate model dates to Fred Rosen-era Ticketmaster in the 1980s, which incentivized venues to sign — [Hypebot](https://www.hypebot.com/ticketmaster-says-we-dont-control-or-keep-most-fees-so-who-does/)
- The March 2026 DOJ–Live Nation settlement caps Ticketmaster service fees at 15% at venues Live Nation owns or controls, limits exclusivity contracts to 4 years, and lets venues and promoters place part of their inventory (up to 50%) on rival platforms — [Live For Live Music](https://liveforlivemusic.com/news/live-nation-settles-doj-antitrust-lawsuit-avoiding-ticketmaster-breakup); [Hypebot / NIVA](https://www.hypebot.com/niva-slams-live-nation-settlement-as-failure-of-the-justice-system)

**Advance vs door [SURVEY-weak/TRADE]**
- Eventbrite's Music Edit 2024, presented at NIVA '24: average purchase-to-show lead time fell 26% from 2022 to 2024. 57% of tickets were sold a week or less before the show in early 2024. This is Eventbrite-platform data only — [Pollstar, Jun 2024](https://news.pollstar.com/2024/06/04/last-minute-ticket-buying-eventbrite-releases-study-at-niva-24/); [Hypebot](https://www.hypebot.com/eventbrite-study-confirms-fans-are-buying-tickets-much-later-than-ever)
- An older ReverbNation venue survey, as cited in a Digital Music News thread, put the median at 75% of tickets sold at the door for 250–1,500-cap venues. It is undated (likely early 2010s) and comes from a reader comment, so treat it as directional only — [DMN](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/?p=2015)

**UK comparator: Music Venue Trust (MVT) [SURVEY/TRADE — UK, not US]**
- 2023 report (2023 data): over £500m in revenue but only £2.5m profit, a **0.5% margin**. The sector would have run at a loss without £3.1m in grants and donations. 125 venues closed (16% of UK grassroots music venues), about 2 a week. Financial non-viability was the leading closure cause for the first time. Rents rose an average of 37%. The 2022 margin was 0.2% — [Music Week, Jan 2024](https://www.musicweek.com/live/read/music-venue-trust-2023-annual-report-reveals-grassroots-closures-now-averaging-two-a-week/089156); [CMU](https://completemusicupdate.com/two-grassroots-venues-close-down-every-week-according-to-new-report-from-music-venue-trust/)
- 2025 report (published Jan 2026):
  - 53.8% of venues made no profit, and the average margin is about 2.5%. Outlets disagree on whether that margin covers all venues or only profitable ones.
  - 30 permanent closures; net venue count fell from 810 to 801.
  - Employment fell from about 31,000 to about 24,000.
  - Attendance rose 13% to 21m.
  - The £1 levy on arena and stadium tickets is voluntary, with a June 2026 deadline before legislation.

  Sources: [Beatportal](https://www.beatportal.com/articles/1252042-nearly-54-percent-of-uk-grassroots-venues-made-no-profit-last-year); [Clash](https://www.clashmusic.com/news/uk-music-venues-lost-6000-jobs-in-2025/); [Tickets for Good](https://www.ticketsforgood.com/uk/blog/inside-the-mvt-report-on-uk-grassroots-music-venues)
- UK: Ticketmaster has given MVT member venues a 50% rebate on booking fees since Nov 2021, with larger benefits for exclusive use. Its earlier per-ticket levy had raised £150k — [Pollstar, Nov 2021](https://news.pollstar.com/2021/11/01/ticketmaster-to-offer-uk-grassroots-music-venues-50-rebate-on-booking-fees/)

### Inferences
- For small venues, ticketing fees are mostly a pass-through, and often a rebate source, rather than a cost. A free ticketing tool's "no fees" pitch therefore saves the fan money but may *remove* a venue revenue line. Venues that rely on fee rebates may see zero-fee ticketing as a loss unless it lets them set their own venue fee.
- The pressures venues actually rank are attendance (23%) and costs. Marketing/awareness ranks low (4%) as an explicit pressure, but "attendance" is partly a discovery problem.
- Last-minute buying (57% within a week, on Eventbrite) and a historically large door share mean any tool that only handles advance sales misses much of the small-show economy. A door/walk-up POS with cash and card reconciliation is load-bearing.

### Gaps
- No US source quantifies what share of a small venue's gross goes to ticketing fees, or what share of US small shows are door-only or cash-only. The ReverbNation 75% figure is old and second-hand.
- NIVA's State of Live PDF (sample size, capacity bands, revenue mix) was not retrieved directly. Figures come from trade summaries.
- There is no US national closure count since 2020, unlike MVT's UK counts.

## 2. Independent artist economics: gig pay, pay-to-play, door splits, delayed or zero payment, merch cuts, travel

### Takeaway
Gig fees for independent artists have been flat or falling while costs have risen. Most surveyed artists lose money or break even on tour (Pirate 2024: 72% no profit; Ditto 2025: about 82–84% say touring is unaffordable). Settlement opacity and late or zero payment at small rooms is a recurring, viral flashpoint, e.g. the Sultan Room $0 settlement in 2025. Merch cuts of 10–35% were partially rolled back at Live Nation clubs in 2023, and unions are now pushing a broader venue pledge (no merch cut, no pay-to-play, no door polling, transparent contracts). The newest US survey data is industry-sponsored. No new MIRA wave since 2018 was found.

### Cited Findings
**Pay and profit [SURVEY]**
- Pirate Studios survey (published July 2024, n=1,700 band members, solo artists and DJs; geography not stated):
  - Gig fees: 54% unchanged and 29% decreased over recent years despite higher ticket prices; 17% increased.
  - Tours: 72% make no profit (48% break even, 24% lose money).
  - 81% report a significant income impact.
  - Biggest costs are travel and lodging, followed by venue fees, crew, food and promotion.

  Source: [Hypebot, Jul 23 2024](https://www.hypebot.com/despite-higher-ticket-prices-musicians-earnings-remain-stagnant.md)
- Ditto Music (2025, n=1,500 independent artists):
  - UK: 84% can't afford to tour, 62.2% turned down tours for financial reasons, and only 16% see touring as feasible — [Ditto press](https://press.dittomusic.com/84-of-independent-artists-in-uk-cant-afford-to-tour-survey-finds)
  - Global framing: 82.1% say touring is too expensive and 58.3% turned down a tour — [A Journal of Musical Things](https://www.ajournalofmusicalthings.com/study-82-of-indie-artists-cant-afford-to-tour-anymore); [Disc Makers via Hypebot, May 2025](https://www.hypebot.com/can-indie-artists-still-afford-to-tour/)
- Ditto 2026 (n>5,000): 74% of independent artists earned under $1,000 from music last year, and 70.6% fund their careers from personal savings — [Hypebot](https://www.hypebot.com/74-of-musicians-earned-under-1k-82-cant-afford-to-tour/)
- MIRA 2018 (the latest wave found; n=1,227 US musicians, 2017 data; with Princeton and MusiCares):
  - Median income was $35,000, of which only $21,300 came from music.
  - 61% said music income doesn't cover living expenses.
  - Live performance was the most common income source.

  Sources: [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/pro/news/the-median-u-s-musician-is-still-making-under-25000-a-year-666833/); [DMN](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2018/06/27/music-industry-research-association-income-study/)
- Comparator (Australia, MEAA 2025, n>300) [SURVEY — not US]: 44% earned under the A$250-per-gig benchmark. One in four performances violated contract terms. 68% were paid for every gig they played, up from 53% in 2020 — [MEAA](https://www.meaa.org/mediaroom/time-for-9-billion-industry-to-pay-up-and-stop-exploiting-professional-musicians/)

**Guarantees, door deals and pay-to-play [VENDOR/TRADE/ANECDOTE]**
- Typical guarantees for independent artists: $100–300 at 50–100-cap bars, $300–800 at 150–300-cap clubs, $800–2,000 at 300–500-cap rooms (vendor guide). Another guide gives $50–200/night for developing acts — [Orphiq](https://orphiq.com/resources/tour-budgeting-independent-artists)
- Pay-to-play is often framed as a "presale". Bands get a ticket allotment and must pay the promoter up front, which "all but eliminat[es] risk" for the promoter — [Performer Mag](https://performermag.com/band-management/contracts-law/pay-to-play-prohibited-or-rite-of-passage)
- Booker Michael Epstein's counterpoint is "good faith" presales, where the band plays even if it sells zero — [Performer Mag](https://performermag.com/band-management/contracts-law/pay-to-play-prohibited-or-rite-of-passage)
- Door-deal mechanics (Australian example): the venue deducts PA and engineer costs (about A$300) plus A$4–5/head. Contracts state 14-day payment, but "the current norm would seem to be more like a month". Ticketing agencies pay in 3–4 days — [CityHub AU](https://cityhub.com.au/?p=251530)

**Delayed, zero or disputed payment [ANECDOTE/TRADE]**
- **Sultan Room, Brooklyn (Aug 2025):** Thor Harris (ex-Swans) posted a settlement showing $0 for a headline show. He said the owner told him "lots of bands get paid nothing… good exposure", and that about a hundred artists then told him they'd been shorted there — [mxdwn, Aug 2025](https://music.mxdwn.com/2025/08/11/news/thor-harris-shares-photo-of-venue-settlement-showing-0-earning-from-recent-nyc-show/)
- **Longmont Social, CO:** the nonprofit Music First said all but one artist it booked had a pay issue. It won its case by default. Other artists were "nervous about joining the claim" — [Longmont Leader](https://longmontleader.com/local-news/music-first-wins-legal-case-against-longmont-social-over-unpaid-performer-10494092)
- **Iridium, NYC:** a settlement was agreed but payment never arrived, and AFM Local 802 leafleted the club — [Local 802](https://local802afm.org/allegro/articles/802-leaflets-employer-and-jazz-artist-gets-paid)

**Merch cuts [TRADE/ANECDOTE]**
- Venue merch cuts reported at 20–25%; artist reps call them "exorbitant" and say they hit small acts hardest — [Billboard (snippet)](https://www.billboard.com/pro/merch-fees-venues-artist-reps-bands-ripped-off)
- #MyMerch (UMAW + Featured Artists Coalition + Cadence Weapon, Nov 2022) says cuts range from 10–35%. About 75 North American venues pledged to be "100% Venues" — [Hypebot, Nov 2022](https://www.hypebot.com/us-musicians-join-mymerch-campaign-to-end-venue-fees-on-merch-sales); [UMAW](https://www.unionofmusicians.org/my-merch)
- Jeff Rosenstock's thread (Labor Day 2023) broke down cuts of 0–20%+ plus tax on his tour. 9:30 Club, Terminal 5, Roadrunner, The Masquerade and Danforth took at least 20% on soft goods. Steve Albini replied that cuts are "100% negotiable" — [Consequence](https://consequence.net/2023/09/jeff-rosenstock-merch-cuts-aeg-live-nation); [Metal Injection](https://metalinjection.net/news/jeff-rosenstock-steve-albini-speak-up-on-venue-merch-cuts)
- Live Nation's "On the Road Again" (Sept 26 2023): about 77 LN-owned clubs and theaters dropped merch fees, and headliners and support acts get a $1,500 per-show stipend. The FADER reported it ran through end-2023, while MBW reported no end date; current status is unverified — [MBW](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/live-nation-scraps-artist-merch-seller-fees-at-its-us-clubs-as-part-of-new-on-the-road-again-program-with-willie-nelson/); [The FADER](https://www.thefader.com/2023/09/26/live-nation-pledges-to-give-up-merch-cuts-at-clubs)
- UMAW "Raise the Bar" (July 2026): 21 venues signed at launch, including The Lab SF, Club Passim and Johnny Brenda's. Venues pledge "no merch cuts, no pay-to-play, no door polling, and greater contract transparency." Door polling means asking attendees who they came to see in order to allocate pay; the article does not define the term — [Hypebot, Jul 8 2026](https://www.hypebot.com/umaws-raise-the-bar-campaign-promotes-fairer-treatment-of-artists-at-venues/)

**Travel [TRADE/ANECDOTE]**
- Garbage (Shirley Manson) said the band would stop bus-touring North America on economics. This is a segment (a) signal, from a 1,448-ticket show grossing $120k — [Pollstar, Dec 2025](https://news.pollstar.com/2025/12/15/you-know-it-aint-easy-the-mixed-bag-of-independent-live-businesses)
- An unverified anecdote describes a Chicago band spending $4,500 on gas, hotels and repairs over 14 days and earning $620. The worked example is illustrative: a $250 venue nut, 30 people at $10, leaves about $50 for the band — [search summary of touring guides](https://www.hypebot.com/can-indie-artists-still-afford-to-tour.md)

### Inferences
- For segment (b), the sharpest *software-solvable* pains are settlement opacity, late or zero payment, and pay-to-play presale accounting. These are problems of information asymmetry and escrow, where a transparent ledger and automatic split payout directly help.
- Gig fees and travel costs are structural (market and fuel) and are not solvable by a platform.
- Merch cuts are a negotiation and policy problem. A platform could help by making merch terms explicit in the deal memo and by running artist-owned merch checkout, but it can't remove the cut.
- The "nervous to complain" dynamic (Longmont) suggests artists value neutral third-party records of what was agreed and what was sold.

### Gaps
- No new MIRA survey since 2018 was found. No UMAW quantitative pay survey was found; UMAW's output is campaigns, not surveys. Future of Music Coalition, MusiCares, Sound Diplomacy and Bandcamp 2024–2026 survey data on gig pay were not located in this pass.
- There is no quantified US prevalence of late or zero payment or pay-to-play. The Australian figure (one in four contracts violated) is the only number found.
- The Billboard merch article is paywalled. Figures come from the snippet only.

## 3. Data ownership: do artists and venues get fan contact data?

### Takeaway
On the major primaries, the ticketing platform and the venue or promoter hold buyer data, and the artist gets "limited reporting." This is now a formal ask from NITO, which wants data sharing written into deal memos, and it appears as an "Artist Transparency" clause in the March 2026 DOJ–Live Nation settlement. Artist-side tools like Bandsintown position "artist owns fan data" as a selling point. No source quantifies the dollar cost of data lock-in.

### Cited Findings
- "When a ticket is sold, the ticketing platform and often the promoter or venue keep the customer information while the artist receives only limited reporting" — [Hypebot on DOJ settlement](https://www.hypebot.com/live-nation-settlement-transparency-clause-is-a-win-for-artists) [TRADE]
- The proposed DOJ settlement's "Artist Transparency" clause lets artists request ticket-buyer info under privacy protections and a DOJ-approved NDA. Commentators doubt Live Nation will comply in practice — [Hypebot](https://www.hypebot.com/live-nation-settlement-transparency-clause-is-a-win-for-artists); [Hypebot: "Will Live Nation share…"](https://www.hypebot.com/will-live-nation-share-fan-ticket-info-with-artists-as-required-in-doj-settlement) [TRADE]
- NITO guest post (Pollstar, Mar 31 2026) [TRADE/position]:
  - "primary ticketing companies place barriers on artists connecting fully with their audiences"
  - "artists are often denied access to the very fan data that would allow them to do that work effectively"
  - Repeated paid social campaigns, often costing "thousands of dollars" to retarget the same audiences, are "increasingly unsustainable."
  - A venue calendar reaches "a million previous ticket buyers" versus an artist's "5,000 loyal fans."
  - NITO urges members to make data sharing part of deal negotiations and offers a model data-share agreement.

  Source: [Pollstar](http://news.pollstar.com/2026/03/31/nito-call-on-ticketers-to-share-buyer-data-with-artists-guest-post/)
- Only Bandsintown "and a handful of other platforms" actively give artists full access to fan data — [Hypebot (commentary)](https://www.hypebot.com/live-nation-settlement-transparency-clause-is-a-win-for-artists) [TRADE]
- Bandsintown's free Fan Management Suite claims artists "own" collected fan contacts, including presale sign-ups regardless of ticketer. A case study says The Head and The Heart captured 4,000+ fan records with email and phone — [Bandsintown blog](https://artists.bandsintown.com/support/blog/meet-our-new-fan-management-suite); [Hypebot 2022](https://www.hypebot.com/bandsintown-gives-artists-total-control-of-their-fan-relationships-at-no-cost/) [VENDOR]
- Ticketmaster's privacy terms treat artists, promoters and venues as independent data controllers. This is a third-party interpretation — [ConductAtlas](https://conductatlas.com/platform/ticketmaster/ticketmaster-privacy-policy/data-sharing-with-event-partners/)
- Fans increasingly "surrender" data at shows. Rolling Stone frames this as a fan privacy concern — [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/concert-rights-as-a-fan-data-sharing-surveillance-1234777970/) [TRADE]
- School vendor GoFan says it doesn't sell customer data but uses buyer emails for its own marketing (opt-out). It does not address school ownership of data — [GoFan FAQ](https://gofan.playonsports.com/faq) [VENDOR]

### Inferences
- Data lock-in is a real, articulated pain for segment (a) and the upper part of (b), artists with managers and agents. NITO is a booking-agent body.
- For small-club artists, the bigger issue is that they have no ticketing relationship at all, because the venue runs the door. For school organizers, the issue is that vendors such as GoFan market to their families.
- A decentralized "artist and organizer own the buyer list" design matches a stated industry ask. But the people asking loudest (agents, NITO) work at scale and are locked into venue-chosen ticketers, so they can't easily adopt a new platform.
- Privacy tension: fans don't necessarily *want* their data shared with every party. Opt-in per-artist consent is the defensible design.

### Gaps
- DICE, Eventbrite, AXS and See Tickets artist data-sharing policies were not retrieved. I found no reliable source on whether DICE shares buyer emails with artists.
- There is no quantified estimate of the revenue cost of data lock-in to artists or venues.
- Ticketmaster's stated position (it shares contact data with venues for cancellations) appears in 2023 Senate QFR responses ([Senate Judiciary](https://www.judiciary.senate.gov/download/2023-01-24-qfr-responses-berchtold?download=1)), but I did not read the document directly.

## 4. Communication friction: advancing, riders, settlement, cancellations, informal contracts

### Takeaway
Evidence is almost entirely anecdotal or how-to guidance. Shows are advanced by email and phone, often within a week of the date. Promoters and venues keep separate calendars. Riders are frequently never seen. Settlements are handwritten or opaque. There is no survey quantifying how often this goes wrong.

### Cited Findings
- Ari Herstand describes a stop where the promoter double-booked the venue with a wedding after 200 advance tickets had sold. He says venue and artist are often "on two separate pages… sometimes with separate calendars", and advises putting everything in the advance email — [Ari's Take](https://aristake.com/how-to-properly-advance-your-shows-and-why-you-have-to/) [ANECDOTE/TRADE]
- A small-venue technical director on ControlBooth says they rarely see an actual rider and usually get the advance less than a week out, with the promoter claiming it "hasn't heard back from the tour" — [ControlBooth forum](https://www.controlbooth.com/threads/concert-promoter-venue-relationship.34648/latest) [ANECDOTE]
- Shows cancelled because a promoter went silent or missed venue deadlines: Clarksville, TN (city statement) — [ClarksvilleNow](https://clarksvillenow.com/?p=242671) [ANECDOTE]
- CD Baby's guide says if a booker doesn't send a contract or detail email, the artist should request it, which implies this commonly doesn't happen — [CD Baby DIY Musician](https://diymusician.cdbaby.com/music-career/advancing-your-show-how-to-confirm-gig-details-ahead-of-time-for-peace-of-mind/) [TRADE]
- Settlement disputes: see the Sultan Room, Longmont Social and Iridium cases in §2. UMAW's "greater contract transparency" plank names "opaque contracts" as a systemic hurdle — [Hypebot](https://www.hypebot.com/umaws-raise-the-bar-campaign-promotes-fairer-treatment-of-artists-at-venues/)
- Comparator (Australia): one in four performances violated contract terms, per MEAA 2025 — [MEAA](https://www.meaa.org/mediaroom/time-for-9-billion-industry-to-pay-up-and-stop-exploiting-professional-musicians/) [SURVEY — not US]

### Inferences
- A shared, versioned "deal memo + advance sheet + settlement" object that both sides see is a clear, low-regulatory-risk gap. It doesn't touch payments law and helps (b) and (c) equally.
- It also creates the paper trail that the Longmont and Iridium disputes lacked.
- Commercial tools exist in this space (e.g., Prism.fm, Master Tour), but none were evaluated in this pass. The writer should check whether a free tool already covers it.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data exists on frequency of advancing failures, rider non-compliance, or settlement disputes in the US.
- I did not retrieve r/WeAreTheMusicMakers or r/Bandmembers threads directly; the search engine returned no Reddit results.

## 5. Promotion friction: social reach, ad costs, discovery, listing fragmentation

### Takeaway
Organic social reach for artists is low (about 2–6% of followers by 2025 estimates). Paid retargeting is called "increasingly unsustainable" by agents. Listings are fragmented across Bandsintown, Songkick, venue calendars and social media. Venues themselves rank marketing/awareness low as a top pressure (4%), but attendance (23%) is their top worry. Hard 2024–2026 data on Facebook Events specifically was not found.

### Cited Findings
- Instagram/Facebook organic reach is about 2–6% of followers (Hypebot, Apr 2025 guide). An older Facebook figure is about 5% — [Hypebot](https://www.hypebot.com/cracking-the-instagram-algorithm-a-2026-guide-for-musicians.md); [Wavo](https://wavo.me/blog/igpaidandorganicpostsdashboard) [TRADE/VENDOR]
- Buffer's analysis of about 43M posts found artist and band Page posts lost 70.6% of engagement after Facebook's friends-first algorithm change, the largest drop of any category. This is pre-2024 (circa 2018) — [Saving Country Music](https://savingcountrymusic.com/?p=109900) [SURVEY-ish, dated]
- NITO: the decline of traditional promotion tools means sales depend on artists' social reach, and repeated paid campaigns cost "thousands of dollars" — [Pollstar, Mar 2026](http://news.pollstar.com/2026/03/31/nito-call-on-ticketers-to-share-buyer-data-with-artists-guest-post/) [TRADE]
- Commenters note bands post dates to Bandsintown and Songkick rather than Facebook Events, which shows the fragmentation — [Hypebot 2015](https://www.hypebot.com/facebook-offers-musicians-an-olive-branch-with-events-subscription-feature/) [ANECDOTE, dated]
- Live Pulse: marketing/awareness is the top pressure for only 4% of independent operators. Ticket sales/attendance is 23% — [Hypebot, Jun 2026](https://www.hypebot.com/niva-bandsintown-survey-tracks-state-of-indie-live-events.md) [SURVEY]
- Luminate: fans' *planned* attendance fell to a three-year low of 31% in Q4 2025 after 36% in Q3 — [Luminate Live Music 2026 via search summary](https://luminatedata.com/reports/live-music-2026/) [SURVEY]

### Inferences
- Discovery for local shows is fragmented, and nothing aggregates small-club, school, church and community events well.
- A decentralized platform's natural strength, open event data that anyone can index, maps to this pain. But discovery has a chicken-and-egg problem and is a poor beachhead on its own.
- It is better as a consequence of capturing event creation, where every ticketed event published becomes an open listing.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 quantitative data on Facebook Events reach or cost per ticket from Meta ads for local shows.
- No survey of how many listing sites a venue or artist must update per show.

## 6. Educational and community organizers: school recitals, college ensembles, community bands, church concerts

### Takeaway
This segment is served by a patchwork:
- K-12 athletics-first platforms (GoFan, Hometown) that are "free to schools" because they charge buyers about $1 + 0–5% per ticket.
- Theater-first platforms (Ludus, On the Stage, BookTix) with similar buyer-paid fees.
- General tools (Eventbrite, about 3.7% + $1.79 + 2.9% processing per paid ticket; free for free events after a reversed 2023 change).
- Donation tools (Zeffy).
- Free-will offerings and volunteer cash boxes.

The documented pains are buyer fees on cheap tickets, fees charged even on cash or comp tickets, equity concerns about digital-only access, cash-handling and audit risk, vendor marketing to families, and confusing fee formats. Programs, recital RSVPs for free events, and livestream are bolted on or missing. The evidence is mostly vendor pages and reviews, and no survey of music educators on ticketing was found.

### Cited Findings
**GoFan (PlayOn) [VENDOR]**
- Buyer fee is $1 for tickets ≤$10, and $1 + 5% of face for tickets over $10. GoFan absorbs card fees and is free for schools. Payout is one weekly deposit on Wednesdays (daily in Washington state). It supports performing arts with reserved seating — [GoFan FAQ](https://gofan.playonsports.com/faq)
- A district page shows a different fee: $1 + 3% (Snohomish, WA) — [Snohomish SD](https://www.sno.wednet.edu/academics/athletics/athletic-admission-prices)
- GoFan uses buyer emails for its own marketing (opt-out) — [GoFan FAQ](https://gofan.playonsports.com/faq)
- GoFan markets concerts, plays and musicals and pitches removing "handling cash, or manual receipt logs." It cites no adoption data — [PlayOn blog](https://blog.playonsports.com/gofan-beyond-sports-turning-every-event-into-a-celebration)

**Hometown Ticketing [VENDOR/TRADE]**
- Its president told the Business Journal it charges $1/ticket with no license or monthly fee. Stripe's 2.9% + 30¢ is separate. No sign-up or annual fees for schools. The article is undated — [Business Journal Daily](https://businessjournaldaily.com/?p=142586)
- Hometown's site claims "no platform fees" and that features, including seat maps and POS, are included in its per-ticket service fee — [Hometown blog](https://hometown.com/blog/why-schools-choose-hometown-box-office-for-digital-ticketing)

**Ludus [VENDOR/REVIEW]**
- It began as Holland (MI) High School's theater ticketing. A competitor lists its fee as 5% + $0.75 per ticket — [Stage Stubs (competitor)](https://stagestubs.com/alternative/ludus)
- Teacher reviews say fees are passed to patrons, so it costs the school nothing; patrons can pay cash to avoid fees. "Administrators have trouble understanding the fee format" and it's "cheaper in most cases but not all" — [Capterra reviews](https://www.capterra.com/p/136136/Ludus/reviews/)

**On the Stage [REVIEW/VENDOR]**
- An unverified per-ticket range of $1.85–3.85 (SoftwareFinder) and a Capterra "starting price $0.85."
- A K-12 reviewer reports being "charged fees on cash ticket sales AND comp tickets" and having to sign a year-long contract.

  Sources: [SoftwareFinder](https://softwarefinder.com/nonprofit/on-the-stage); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.ca/reviews/1038009/on-the-stage)
- It offers digital program fees folded into ticket price for its streaming product — [Playbill](https://m.playbill.com/article/hunter-arnold-expands-remote-production-platform-for-streaming-theatre)

**BookTix [VENDOR]**
- "Free to your organization for every ticket you sell online," with no setup or subscription. This is a partner page; buyer-fee terms are not stated, and it may be UK-focused — [Theatrical Rights Worldwide UK](https://www.theatricalrights.co.uk/types/ticketing/)

**Other school or nonprofit tools [VENDOR]**
- TutuTix (dance recitals): $1/seat + 5% card processing to buyers — [Dance Teacher](https://dance-teacher.com/?p=2421)
- Vanco/RevTrak Events: seating charts and printed tickets for school performing arts — [Vanco slides](https://www.revtrak.com/hubfs/Educational%20Market/Vanco%20Events/Vanco_Events_for_Performing_Arts_Slides.pdf)

**Eventbrite (general tool many schools and community groups use) [VENDOR/TRADE]**
- In late 2023 it added organizer fees on free events over 25 attendees, a change that drew backlash and was reversed in 2024. A competitor puts the reputational and financial hit at about $20M — [Checkout Page](https://checkoutpage.com/blog/eventbrite-fees); [Ticket Tailor](https://www.tickettailor.com/blog/bending-spoons-acquires-eventbrite-what-does-this-mean)
- Current fees per competitor blogs: 3.7% + $1.79 per paid ticket plus 2.9% processing — [TickPick (competitor)](https://www.tickpick.com/blog/organizer/eventbrite-2026-fee-changes)

The chair said digital-only access and added cost raise equity issues for families without reliable internet or credit cards: "It shifts the playing field a little bit for parents, caregivers, and students" — [Andover News](https://andovermanews.com/?p=15195)
- Seattle Public Schools audits found funds not deposited within the 24-hour window required by state law and the ASB manual. They also warned that booster money mixed into school safes becomes public money — [SPS audit](https://www.seattleschools.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/RBHS-Final-Internal-Audit-Report-ADA-Compliant.pdf); [Nathan Hale audit](https://www.seattleschools.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/Final-Nathan-Hale-Report-ADA-Compliant.pdf)
- Booster-fund audit findings elsewhere: a single unsupervised person handling deposits (St. Charles East, IL), missing funds (Galt, CA), and $124k in off-book JROTC deposits (Bearden HS, TN, 2026) — [Daily Herald](https://www.dailyherald.com/20110622/news/d303-audit-shows-accounting-flaws-no-proof-of-crime/); [CBS Sacramento](https://www.cbsnews.com/sacramento/news/liberty-ranch-high-school-athletic-booster-club-funds-missing); [Hoodline](https://hoodline.com/2026/03/bearden-high-jrotc-busted-over-secret-124k-bank-stash/)

**Community bands, orchestras and church series (practice patterns) [ANECDOTE — event pages]**
- Free reserved tickets online plus a door box office opening 2 hours before (Westport Community Band) — [HappeningNext](https://happeningnext.com/event/westport-community-band-free-concert-eid3a0dum97f0)
- Free park concert where online "free" tickets carry a processing fee unless picked up in person (Eugene Symphony) — [Eugene Chamber](https://web.eugenechamber.com/events/Eugene-Symphony-in-the-Park-Cuthbert-Amphitheater-2315516/details)
- Community orchestra pricing of $15 advance / $18 door, free for under-12s and college students, with an optional donation at checkout (Oklahoma Community Orchestra on ThunderTix) — [ThunderTix event](https://oklahomacommunityorchestra.thundertix.com/events/252727)
- Free concerts with food-pantry donations instead of tickets (Niles, MI) — [ABC57](https://www.abc57.com/news/niles-orchestra-seeing-growth)
- Volunteers staff box office and scanning in exchange for free tickets (Austin Symphonic Band, Johns Creek Symphony, Fargo-Moorhead Symphony) — [GiveGab](https://www.givegab.com/nonprofits/austinsymphonicband/opportunities/austin-symphonic-band-volunteer-details); [FM Symphony](https://www.fmsymphony.org/volunteer)
- Church series combine free-will offerings (e.g., a suggested $20 to the music fund), livestream, QR or text-to-give, and in some cases paid livestream tickets ($20/device) — [Westminster Presbyterian](https://wpc.org/event/westminster-choir-spring-concert-2024/); [IHM](https://www.ihmchurchmd.org/ihm-concert-series); [Beatitudes UCC](https://swcucc.org/church-news-events-blog/2021/10/2/euphonium-dynamism-at-beatitudes)
- College: UNT orchestra runs a complimentary-ticket policy for students and performers, which shows comp allocation is a workflow need — [UNT](https://music.unt.edu/orchestra/student-information/complimentary-tickets.html)

### Inferences
- **The fee structure is regressive for this segment.** A flat $1 on a $5–10 school concert ticket is a 10–20% surcharge, versus about 3–5% on a $40 club ticket. On the Stage reportedly charges fees on comps and cash. Families notice, as the Andover equity complaint shows.
- Vendors call themselves "free to schools" because the cost is shifted to parents.
- **Gaps the incumbents leave:**
  - Free-RSVP capacity management for free recitals. Eventbrite works but markets to attendees and its terms are volatile post-acquisition.
  - Integrated digital programs. Only On the Stage bundles them, for streaming.
  - Donation and free-will collection alongside tickets.
  - Clean cash-box reconciliation that satisfies district audit rules such as 24-hour deposit and two-person counts.
  - Comp allocation for performers' families.
  - Buyer data that stays with the school.
- This segment has three properties that make it a plausible beachhead:
  1. Low ticket prices and price-sensitive families, where zero-fee pricing is most valuable.
  2. A reachable distribution channel: music educators, NAfME, state MEAs, and conferences Ben presents at.
  3. Simple events. One night, general admission or simple reserved seating, no resale, no artist guarantees.
- **The main adoption barrier is district procurement.** District policy may mandate a vendor, as with MIAA for athletics. Ludus- and GoFan-type platforms already offer "free to school." So the pitch must be "free to *families* too" plus programs, RSVP and audit-clean cash handling, not just "free."

### Gaps
- Official current fee schedules for Ludus, On the Stage, Arts People, BookTix (US) and Hometown were not retrieved. Several vendor pages returned 403 or 404, and figures above are secondhand.
- There is no survey of music educators' ticketing practices, i.e. the share using paper, Google Forms, Eventbrite, GoFan and so on. The r/MusicEd threads were not surfaced by search.
- Arts People and Vendini (legacy) were not researched.
- No data on how many school concerts are free versus ticketed, or on the prevalence of district-mandated vendors for fine arts (as opposed to athletics).

## 7. Fan-side pain that artists absorb reputationally

### Takeaway
Fees, bots, speculative or "ghost" listings and resale markups dominate federal policy activity in 2025–2026: the FTC all-in pricing rule (May 2025), Executive Order 14254 (March 2025), the TICKET Act, and the DOJ settlement. Cost is the top barrier to attendance (53% of the US public in Q1 2026). This is mostly a segment (a) problem. Small clubs and school events have little resale or bot activity, but fans carry fee resentment to every checkout.

### Cited Findings
- Luminate: 53% of the US general population cite ticket cost as a barrier (Q1 2026), down from 59% in Q1 2024. Among Gen Z it is 57%, down from 75% two years earlier. Top-100 tour average ticket was $127.17 in 2025 versus $130.36 in 2024 — [Luminate Live Music 2026](https://luminatedata.com/reports/live-music-2026/); [Barrett Media](https://barrettmedia.com/2026/07/01/live-music-revenue-plateaus-after-years/) [SURVEY]
- EO 14254 (Mar 31 2025) targets bots and "unscrupulous middlemen… egregious fees" and cites fans paying up to 70× face. It orders FTC enforcement of the BOTS Act; the FTC had enforced it only once in about 8 years — [UCSB Presidency Project](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/executive-order-14254-combating-unfair-practices-the-live-entertainment-market); [KQED](https://www.kqed.org/arts/13973879/trump-ticket-scalping-executive-order) [TRADE]
- Kid Rock: "You can buy a ticket for $100. By the time you check out, it's $170"; set prices low and "bots buy them up instantly" — [ClickOnDetroit/AP](https://www.clickondetroit.com/news/politics/2025/03/31/trump-targets-ticket-scalpers-and-high-live-event-fees-which-he-calls-price-gouging/) [ANECDOTE]
- The FTC Rule on Unfair or Deceptive Fees has required all-in price display for live-event tickets since May 12 2025. It doesn't cap fees. StubHub paid a $10M settlement as the first major enforcement — [Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2024/12/17/ftc-mandates-all-in-pricing-as-ticket-act-gets-lame-duck-life-with-surprising-pushback/); [JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/ftc-v-stubhub-10-million-settlement-8021470/) [TRADE]
- TICKET Act: the House passed it 409–15 on Apr 30 2025. NIVA and NITO flagged a "concierge service" loophole in the speculative-ticket ban and fee-hiding risk — [CMU](https://completemusicupdate.com/us-house-passes-ticket-act-but-industry-groups-raise-concerns-about-loophole-in-speculative-selling-ban/) [TRADE]
- NIVA warned that the DOJ settlement's requirement for Ticketmaster to host resale listings "would likely exacerbate the price gouging potential for predatory resellers" — [Hypebot](https://www.hypebot.com/niva-slams-live-nation-settlement-as-failure-of-the-justice-system) [TRADE]

### Inferences
- For segments (b) through (d), bots and dynamic pricing are largely irrelevant. Shows don't sell out fast enough to attract them.
- What does transfer is **fee resentment** and **app-only/transfer friction**. A small-show tool should show all-in prices (now federally required anyway), allow simple transfers, and work without an app (printable/PDF or name-at-door).
- Anti-bot and resale-cap features are segment (a) problems and a poor beachhead for a small open-source project.

### Gaps
- No data on app-only ticket complaints or transfer restrictions at small venues specifically.
- The outcome of the state-led trial that continued after the DOJ settlement, and the judge's Tunney Act ruling, were not found. As of Sep 2026, NIVA asked the court to reject the settlement.

## 8. What artists, venues and advocates say they WANT

### Takeaway
The stated asks fall into four groups:
- **Price and fee transparency and anti-speculation** (Fix the Tix/NIVA).
- **Structural antitrust remedies**: break up Live Nation–Ticketmaster, cap tour share (NIVA).
- **Buyer-data sharing with artists** (NITO; DOJ "Artist Transparency" clause).
- **Fair venue practices for small artists**: no merch cut, no pay-to-play, no door polling, transparent contracts (UMAW).

No surveyed group asked for "a new ticketing platform" per se. They ask for transparency, ownership and fairness.

### Cited Findings
- Fix the Tix (NIVA-led coalition, March 2026 letter to Senate Commerce) asks for:
  - Base price and every fee disclosed from first selection through checkout.
  - A complete ban on speculative tickets with no concierge or "seat saver" exception.
  - Resale capped at the original total price, and resale fees capped at 10%.

  Source: [NIVA statement](https://www.nivassoc.org/statements/2026/independent-music-and-live-entertainment-industry-urges-senate-commerce-leaders-to-strengthen-ticket-act-with-complete-fake-ticket-ban-resale-price-cap-and-full-price-transparency); [Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2026/03/05/fix-the-tix-pushes-for-ticket-act-change-including-10-resale-cap/)
- Fix the Tix in Feb 2025 had 30+ organizations, including NIVA, NITO, RIAA and Bandsintown — [Hypebot](https://www.hypebot.com/hypebot/2025/02/ticket-act-would-legalize-ghost-tickets.html)
- NIVA on the DOJ settlement:
  - The $280M payment "is the equivalent of 4 days of their 2025 revenue" and the settlement is "a failure of the justice system."
  - It later asked the judge to reject the settlement in favour of structural remedies, including Ticketmaster divestiture and a 50% tour cap.

  Sources: [Hypebot](https://www.hypebot.com/niva-slams-live-nation-settlement-as-failure-of-the-justice-system); [NIVA](https://www.nivassoc.org/statements/2026/national-independent-venue-association-tells-judge-to-reject-the-proposed-doj-live-nation-settlement)
- NITO: make data sharing part of every deal negotiation, using its model data-share agreement — [Pollstar](http://news.pollstar.com/2026/03/31/nito-call-on-ticketers-to-share-buyer-data-with-artists-guest-post/)
- UMAW "Raise the Bar": "no merch cuts, no pay-to-play, no door polling, and greater contract transparency" — [Hypebot, Jul 2026](https://www.hypebot.com/umaws-raise-the-bar-campaign-promotes-fairer-treatment-of-artists-at-venues/)
- UMAW #MyMerch asks venues to register as "100% Venues" — [UMAW](https://www.unionofmusicians.org/my-merch)
- UMAW's Living Wage for Musicians Act was reintroduced Sept 29 2025. It is streaming-focused, not live — [Hypebot](https://www.hypebot.com/rashida-tlaib-reintroduce-the-living-wage-for-musicians-act/)
- MVT (UK comparator) wants a £1 levy on arena and stadium tickets to fund grassroots venues — [Hypebot](https://www.hypebot.com/us-independent-music-venues-are-in-crisis-and-the-uk-has-a-solution/)
- Venue-side "want": diversified revenue, starting with membership programs that turn fans into recurring supporters (TSE/Hypebot) — [Hypebot](https://www.hypebot.com/why-ticket-sales-arent-enough-for-independent-music-venues-anymore)

### Inferences
- Two of the four ask clusters are things software can embody by design, without legislation:
  - **Transparency**: all-in pricing, visible fee split, visible settlement.
  - **Data ownership**: organizer- and artist-owned buyer lists.
- A free, open-source platform could make UMAW's "Raise the Bar" terms the default template: merch-cut field defaulting to 0%, shared settlement sheet, no presale-purchase obligation. Pledging venues (about 21 to 75) are a ready-made early-adopter list for segment (b)/(c).
- Venue "membership programs" suggest recurring-support primitives (memberships and donations) matter as much as single tickets for (c) and (d).

### Gaps
- Break Up Ticketmaster campaign materials and artist open letters (2024–2026) were not retrieved in this pass.
- No survey asks artists or venues to rank desired features of a ticketing tool.

## 9. Ranked pain catalog by segment, and beachhead implications

### Takeaway
Ranked by frequency × strength of evidence, the most acute and most software-addressable pains are:
- **Small-artist settlement opacity and late or zero pay** (segment b).

- **Buyer-data lock-in** (segments a and upper b).

Fan-side bots and dynamic pricing are the loudest pains overall but belong to segment (a) and are policy problems. Segment (d) is the most underserved relative to its reachability and its fit with "free."

**Most underserved demographics:**
1. **K-12 and community music organizers (d):** low-price tickets make flat fees proportionally worst. The work is done by volunteers or teachers with no box-office staff, audit exposure is real, and incumbents are athletics- or theater-first. Ben can reach this segment directly.
2. **Independent artists at clubs and bars (b):** they have no contractual leverage, no data, and opaque settlement. Their pains are mostly information problems that a neutral ledger fixes.
3. **Small venues (c)** are financially the most fragile. But they are locked into ticketer rebate deals and need door, bar and membership integration. A free platform competes with rebate income they currently receive, which makes them harder first adopters.

**Beachhead implications (inference, for the report writer):**
- Segment (d) has the best fit of pain, reachability and simplicity. The fit is strongest around free or low-cost recitals, school concerts, and community and church series, with:
  - zero fees to families
  - free-RSVP capacity control
  - comps
  - cash/card door reconciliation with an audit export
  - digital programs
  - donations or free-will offerings
  - school-owned audience lists
- Segment (b)'s "transparent deal → settlement → automatic split" is the strongest *second* wedge. It aligns with UMAW's pledge venues as early adopters.
- Avoid leading with segment (a) features (anti-bot, resale caps, dynamic pricing). They are policy-driven and dominated by incumbents.

### Gaps
- The ranking is qualitative. No cross-segment survey measures pain intensity on a common scale.
- Segment (d) evidence is the thinnest in survey terms. A short survey of Ben's music-educator network, covering current tool, fees paid, cash handling and missing features, would be the cheapest falsification probe before committing to it as the beachhead.
