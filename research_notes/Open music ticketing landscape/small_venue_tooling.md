# Small/Mid-Venue Ticketing, Booking, Settlement & Promotion Tooling (US, as of Oct 2026)

Research date: 2026-10-14. Scope: US venues under ~2,500 cap, independent promoters, independent artists. Many fee figures come from vendor pages or competitor blogs; every figure below says which. Several vendors (DICE, Etix, Tixr, Prekindle, TicketWeb, Opendate ticketing, Prism) **do not publish US rate cards**. That opacity is itself a finding.

## 1. Ticketing for small/mid venues: fees, data ownership, exclusivity, payouts

### Takeaway
The market splits in two. (a) **Self-serve, published-price** platforms: Eventbrite, Ticket Tailor, Humanitix, VenuePilot, TickPick, pretix.cloud. They have no contract, and fees run from flat $0.75/ticket up to 3.7% + $1.79 + 2.9% processing. (b) **Sales-led, contract** platforms: Etix, TicketWeb (Live Nation), DICE (Fever), Tixr, Prekindle, Eventim (ex-See Tickets US), Opendate ticketing. They use negotiated fee schedules, often with fee rebates to the venue, advances/signing bonuses, and exclusivity. In 2025–26 the market consolidated further: Fever bought DICE, Bending Spoons bought Eventbrite, Eventim absorbed See Tickets US, and ShowClix became Leap. A jury also found Live Nation/Ticketmaster liable as a monopolist in April 2026.

### Cited Findings

**Published-price / self-serve platforms**
- **Eventbrite (official page, fetched 2026-10-14):** "3.7% + $1.79 service fee per ticket" (paid tickets only) plus "2.9% payment processing fee per order". Free events cost nothing. Buyers pay by default, and organizers can choose to absorb. Eventbrite Pro (email marketing) is "Starting at $15/month". The page lists "Pre-event payouts" as a feature but gives no timing. — [Eventbrite pricing](https://www.eventbrite.com/organizer/pricing/)
- Eventbrite effective rate (third-party calc): about 15.6% on a $20 ticket, ~11% on $40, ~8.4% on $100. Pro tiers run $15–$100/mo and do not cut per-ticket fees. Nonprofits get 50% off Pro. One guide lists conflicting older tiers (2% + $0.79 Essentials / 3.5% + $1.59 Professional), probably stale. — [TickPick: Eventbrite 2026 changes](https://www.tickpick.com/blog/organizer/eventbrite-2026-fee-changes); [Posh 2026 breakdown](https://posh.vip/university/post/eventbrite-fees-explained-2026)
- **Eventbrite ownership change:** Bending Spoons signed a definitive agreement in Dec 2025, an "all-cash deal valued at approximately $500 million", which closed March 2026. Eventbrite is now private and delisted. — [SquadUP 2026 landscape (vendor blog, 2026-09-29)](https://blog.squadup.com/the-2026-live-event-technology-landscape-whats-changed-and-what-matters-now); [Wikipedia: Eventbrite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eventbrite)
- **Ticket Tailor:** flat per-ticket fee with no percentage. Pay-as-you-go is £0.60 + VAT (~$0.85 USD) per ticket, falling toward ~$0.30 / £0.22 with prepaid credits. Assigned seats cost ~$1.70 each. Stripe/PayPal/Square processing is extra. There is a 50% charity discount. White-label runs ~£39 / $49/mo. Free tickets are free up to ~5,000/yr (sources conflict). All figures are third-party. — [Dupple review (Jul 2026)](https://dupple.com/reviews/ticket-tailor); [Rework comparison](https://resources.rework.com/tools/events/eventbrite-vs-ticket-tailor); [TickPick small-venue guide (2026-08-16)](https://www.tickpick.com/blog/organizer/best-ticketing-platform-small-venues)
- **Humanitix (non-profit, profits to charity; US HQ Denver):** US page shows Standard at **2.1% + $0.99 per paid ticket** and Charities & Schools at **1% + $0.99**, with processing listed separately at **2.9% + $0.30**. Free events are free. "NO sign up fees, NO contracts." The page doesn't say who pays by default. A July 2026 Humanitix post says fees are unchanged effective 1 July 2026 and have not risen in 10 years. — [Humanitix US pricing](https://humanitix.com/us/pricing); [Humanitix blog 2026-07-13 (CA site)](https://humanitix.com/ca/blog/how-our-booking-fees-are-changing); [Colorado gov: Denver HQ](https://www.colorado.gov/governor/news/7141-humanitix-selects-denver-us-headquarters). Note: Wikipedia states "5% plus a flat per-ticket charge inclusive of processing" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanitix)). That conflicts with the US pricing page, and the official page should win.
- **VenuePilot (Brooklyn, founded 2016, built for independent venues):** **$0.75 per online ticket**, under a "Low Fee Guarantee" that it "will never go up". Door, walk-up, phone and label sales carry no platform fee, and any fees the venue adds are "100% yours to keep". It runs on the **venue's own Stripe account** (Stripe's published rate applies) with **daily payouts**. "No setup fees and no contract". It is "Never exclusive. Never binding." Core software (calendar, holds, offer-sheet generator, settlements, staff scheduling, free venue website and widgets) is described as free. — [VenuePilot pricing](https://venuepilot.com/pricing); [VenuePilot home](https://venuepilot.com/)
- VenuePilot "UpFront": a revenue-based advance (not a loan) that renews annually. Tickets carry a "slightly higher per-ticket fee" until it is repaid. "No exclusivity. No 2-year lockup." — [VenuePilot pricing](https://venuepilot.com/pricing); [UpFront](https://upfront.venuepilot.com/)
- **TickPick (organizer side):** 5% plus processing (~2.9% + $0.30). Payouts come the Wednesday after the event, or weekly "Lightning" payouts. No monthly fee or term. — [TickPick guide (self-promotional, ranks itself #1)](https://www.tickpick.com/blog/organizer/best-ticketing-platform-small-venues)
- **TicketSpice:** $0.99 flat, or $0.49 on tickets ≤ $5 (competitor blog figure). — [TickPick fees explained](https://www.tickpick.com/blog/organizer/ticketing-fees-explained)

**Sales-led / contract platforms**
- **Etix:** sales-led and negotiated per venue, with no public rate. Examples range from a venue adding its own fee while paying Etix little per ticket, to a percentage plus several dollars. A competitor blog estimates "$1–2/ticket + processing, next-business-day payouts" (unverified). Etix calls itself the largest independent ticketing company in North America, with a sweet spot of venues ≤ 12,000 seats. — [TickPick guide](https://www.tickpick.com/blog/organizer/best-ticketing-platform-small-venues); [Yapsody Eventbrite alternatives (competitor)](https://www.yapsody.com/ticketing/blog/eventbrite-alternatives/); [Business NC on Etix (2026-02-16)](https://businessnc.com/nc-trend-etix-rocking-the-events-biz/)
- **TicketWeb:** contract-based with no published fee. It plugs venues into the Ticketmaster network and is owned by Ticketmaster's parent (Live Nation). — [TickPick guide](https://www.tickpick.com/blog/organizer/best-ticketing-platform-small-venues)
- **Universe:** Ticketmaster bought it (with Front Gate) in 2016. Ticketmaster positions it as a "self-serve ticketing solution built for independent venues, clubs, and promoters". No shutdown found. — [MBW](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/ticketmaster-acquires-front-gate-tickets-and-universe/); [Ticketmaster UK business page](https://business.ticketmaster.co.uk/?p=59792)
- **DICE:** acquired by Fever (announced ~June 5, 2025, terms undisclosed). Onboarding is invite-gated, with no published US pricing. Fan-facing booking fees are "generally cited around 10%" (TickPick). A competitor (Sway) claims 5–12% per ticket, payouts held until post-event, and DICE retention of customer contacts. These are **competitor claims, unverified**. DICE's 2026 US signings skew to nightclubs and electronic venues: Saint Vitus (Brooklyn), ViViD Presents (PNW), The Originals / Phase 3 / Studio 60 (Miami), Framework and Sound Nightclub (LA), Factory Town & Club Space (Miami), Public Records (NYC). — [Pollstar 2026-07-03](https://news.pollstar.com/2026/07/03/fever-dice-reflect-on-first-year-of-partnership/); [Music Week](https://www.musicweek.com/live/read/dice-announces-new-wave-of-us-partnerships/094779); [Sway (competitor)](https://www.sway.events/blog/best-dice-alternatives-promoters); [TickPick guide](https://www.tickpick.com/blog/organizer/best-ticketing-platform-small-venues)
- **Tixr:** custom contract via a sales call. An SEC-filed sample Tixr services agreement shows a **commission tiered by ticket face value** (amounts redacted) plus card processing "based upon the 'all-in' ticket price". Comps carry no fee, and a "Rewards FEE WAIVED (normally 1.9%)" line appears. — [SEC EX-10.1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1770501/000164117225026982/ex10-1.htm); [Ticket Fairy Eventbrite vs Tixr](https://www.ticketfairy.com/event-ticketing/eventbrite-vs-tixr.md)
- **Prekindle:** no public fees. There are three tiers (DIY, Professional, Custom), and buyers must contact the vendor. — [Capterra UK](https://www.capterra.co.uk/software/202877/prekindle)
- **See Tickets US → "Eventim":** CTS Eventim acquired See Tickets in June 2024, and US operations were rebranded Eventim (Pollstar, 2025-03-04). Earlier, See Tickets had courted indies, signing 100 indie venues/promoters in 12 months by Apr 2021, including the Troubadour. — [Pollstar 2025-03-04](https://news.pollstar.com/2025/03/04/see-tickets-us-rebranded-as-eventim-following-2024-acquisition/); [Pollstar 2021](https://news.pollstar.com/2021/04/22/see-tickets-welcomes-100-indie-venues-promoter-clients-doubling-roster-over-last-12-months/); [Wikipedia: See Tickets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/See_Tickets)
- **ShowClix → Leap Event Technology:** in April 2026, ShowClix and Ticketbooth dropped their names and now operate as "Leap Event Technology", with products unchanged. Pricing is usage-based (contact vendor). Capterra rates it 4.0/5 overall but **3.2/5 on value for money** (20 reviews). Customers skew to conventions, attractions, festivals and music/comedy venues. — [Leap announcement](https://leapevent.tech/en-au/?p=8152); [Capterra ShowClix](https://www.capterra.com/p/82542/Easy-To-Use-Ticketing-Software)
- **Opendate ticketing:** fee schedules vary by contract. Ticketing customers get no fee for the venue-management software, website or mobile app. Opendate's own benchmark report puts the **average organizer ticket fee at 6.6% (range 0%–24.6%)**. A "round-up" feature rounds organizer fees to the next whole dollar (+$0.62/ticket in one case study). — [TrustRadius Opendate](https://www.trustradius.com/products/opendate); [The Ticketing Business on Opendate benchmark](https://www.theticketingbusiness.com/?p=97679); [Opendate Forty5 case study](https://www.opendate.io/case-study/how-forty5-generated-80-000-in-new-revenue-with-round-up-pricing)

**Contract economics: advances, rebates, exclusivity**
- Water & Music (2020-11-30), a pre-pandemic snapshot: platforms gave rising venues "hundreds of thousands of dollars" in signing bonuses. Example: a 1,000-cap room with 8 shows/mo gets a $100k bonus for a 1-year deal plus an extra 15% fee, earning the platform ~$230k, about 130% implied interest. Signing bonuses and advances were 21% of Eventbrite's $368M expenses the prior year. Mid/large venues often take **up to 80% of the listed service fee** as a rebate ("facility charge"). — [Water & Music](https://www.waterandmusic.com/signing-bonuses-used-to-run-the-ticketing-business-what-happens-now)
- Exclusive multi-venue deals continue, e.g. First Avenue (Minneapolis, 6 venues) and JAM Productions (Chicago) with AXS. — [IAVM: AXS–First Avenue](https://blog.iavm.org/axs-and-first-avenue-sign-exclusive-deal/); [IAVM: JAM–AXS](https://blog.iavm.org/legendary-concert-promoter-jam-productions-signs-exclusive-ticketing-deal-with-axs/)
- Prism's own example: a club selling 50,000 tickets/yr pays a ticketing partner ~$75,000/yr at $1.50/ticket, and "some ticketing contracts are up to 40x" Prism's $7,500/yr fee. — [Prism vs Opendate (vendor page)](https://prism.fm/opendate/)

**Regulatory / market context**
- The FTC Rule on Unfair or Deceptive Fees took effect **May 12, 2025** for live-event tickets. It requires all-in total price up front, more prominent than partial prices, and it covers resale. It does **not cap fees**. Civil penalties are up to $53,088 per violation (inflation-indexed). — [Barnes & Thornburg](https://btlaw.com/en/insights/alerts/2025/ftc-finalizes-junk-fees-rule-new-pricing-disclosure-requirements-take-effect-may-12-2025); [Perkins Coie](https://perkinscoie.com/insights/update/ftc-junk-fee-rule-here-along-wave-state-laws)
- Live Nation antitrust: DOJ settled mid-trial. The settlement opens ticketing at 13 amphitheaters, bars retaliation against venues that decline Ticketmaster, and creates a $280M pot for states, pending court approval. A jury verdict for 33 states + DC on **April 15, 2026** found illegal monopolies in primary ticketing for 200+ major concert venues and in large amphitheaters, and an overcharge of $1.72/ticket in 22 states. Remedies (possible divestiture) were with Judge Subramanian (S.D.N.Y.); status after April is not confirmed. — [The Ticketing Business 2026-04](https://www.theticketingbusiness.com/2026/04/jury-rules-live-nation-ticketmaster-held-monopoly-in-landmark-case/); [Thompson Coburn](https://www.thompsoncoburn.com/insights/live-nation-and-ticketmaster-found-liable-for-antitrust-violations-by-federal-jury/)
- Kustom Entertainment (Nasdaq) agreed in Sept 2026 to acquire Tickets For Less (a wholesale ticket distribution platform). This is an agreement only, per a vendor blog citing an SEC exhibit. — [SquadUP](https://blog.squadup.com/the-2026-live-event-technology-landscape-whats-changed-and-what-matters-now)

### Inferences
- On a typical $15–$25 club ticket, Eventbrite's flat $1.79 dominates the cost (~12–16% all-in). Flat-fee players (VenuePilot $0.75, Ticket Tailor ~$0.85, Humanitix $0.99 + 2.1%) are 2–4x cheaper for the venue. An open-source alternative's pitch is therefore not "cheaper than Eventbrite", which VenuePilot already is, but zero platform fee, portable data, and no counterparty risk.
- The contract tier's real lock-in is financial (advances/signing bonuses repaid through fees, plus multi-year exclusivity), not technical. Venues also *profit* from fee rebates, so "low fees" alone may not win them. A credible alternative needs a story for venue-retained fees, which the FTC all-in rule permits if disclosed, and possibly for working capital.
- The VenuePilot pattern (venue's own Stripe account, daily payouts, non-exclusive, flat fee) is the closest existing commercial analog to what an OSS/decentralized design would offer. It is the benchmark to beat.
- Bending Spoons' ownership history suggests price and staff changes at Eventbrite are plausible. This is speculation, and no 2026 Eventbrite fee change was confirmed beyond the 3.7% + $1.79 + 2.9% structure.

### Gaps
- No primary, current fee schedules for DICE, Etix, TicketWeb, Tixr, Prekindle, Eventim US, Universe, Freshtix, Eventix, or Leap. All are contract-only. Competitor blog figures are flagged above.
- Data-ownership clauses were not verified in any vendor's actual ToS. VenuePilot and Opendate *market* "100% ownership" of customer data, but the contract terms were not read. Whether DICE gives venues buyer emails is unverified (only a competitor claim exists).
- Freshtix, Eventix (Dutch, likely irrelevant to US), Squarespace/Stripe DIY, and Bandcamp ticketing were not researched in this pass. No source found on Bandcamp offering ticketing.
- Payout timing for Eventbrite (beyond "pre-event payouts" as a feature), Humanitix, and Ticket Tailor was not confirmed from primary pages.
- Post-April 2026 status of Live Nation remedies and the DOJ settlement approval is unknown.

## 2. Venue operations / booking / settlement software

### Takeaway
Booking/settlement is dominated by **Prism.fm** (booking-only SaaS, subscription, ticketing-agnostic) and **Opendate** (an all-in-one where ticketing fees subsidize free booking software, $14M Series A Jan 2026, ~200+ operators). **VenuePilot** bundles free booking/settlement with $0.75 ticketing. Most offer holds, offer sheets, deal statuses and settlement sheets. None publishes a portable data format for offers, holds or settlements.

### Cited Findings
- **Prism.fm** (vendor's own claims): deal statuses run "Draft Offer" to "Contract Signed". It has bulk hold/challenge management, settlements for multi-night events, co-pros, tours, multiple fee types, deal structures, adjustments and ancillary revenue, plus deposit, settlement and withholding tracking. Also included: forecasts and profitability reports, email/text templates, an Advance tool, tasks, files, Slack webhooks, and granular permissions. It reports **>10,000 offers sent per week (2025)**. It does not charge per ticket. Its example club pays **$7,500/yr**, about $0.15/ticket. Some ticketing partners pay customers' Prism subscriptions. Prism integrates with "a growing number of ticketing partners" (unnamed). — [Prism vs Opendate](https://prism.fm/opendate/)
- Prism's criticisms of Opendate (competitor claims): Opendate is "primarily a ticketing company". It shares ownership with and is closely tied to venue operator/promoter **Forty5**. Its low-cost booking features "funnel leads to its ticketing platform". It has no integrations outside its own ticketing. — [Prism vs Opendate](https://prism.fm/opendate/)
- **Opendate:** a **US$14M Series A on 2026-01-28** led by High Alpha. It serves **200+ independent operators** after doubling its customer base in a year. Products cover booking (offer-to-confirmation), integrated ticketing, email/SMS marketing automation, analytics, and "Conductor", an AI workspace. It claims venues "own their customer data", with no terms specified. The revenue model is subscription plus payment-processing fees (Caplight). Capterra says "contact vendor". — [ContentGrip](https://www.contentgrip.com/opendate-series-a-live-event-marketing-platform/); [Caplight](https://www.caplight.com/company/opendate-inc); [Capterra](https://capterra.com/p/10003034/Opendate/)
- **VenuePilot:** centralized booking, hold management, automated advancing, an offer sheet generator, settlement sheets for "complex deal structures" and multi-date events, artist payment tracking, staff scheduling, and unlimited users/venues. Core software is free, and it integrates with Hive (email marketing). — [VenuePilot pricing](https://venuepilot.com/pricing); [Hive partnership](https://hive.co/blog/hive-partners-with-venuepilot-to-empower-independent-venues)
- **Gigwell** (agency/artist-side booking): $33–$399/mo flat-rate per Capterra. Reviews call it "well thought out for Booking Agents" but cumbersome at volume, and one reviewer calls payment processing "a huge challenge". — [Capterra Gigwell](https://www.capterra.ca/software/162663/gigwell)
- **Master Tour (Eventric):** a tour management platform covering personnel, itineraries, venue details, day sheets, guest lists, setlists, accounting and tasks (2023 description). The 2019 model was a monthly desktop subscription plus a free mobile-only version. No 2026 pricing was found. — [Eventric press clipping](https://eventric.rockpaperscissors.biz/artist/9906/press_clips/20124/white)
- **Bandsintown for venues/promoters:** venue page claim is free. Promoted Email campaigns cost a minimum of **$150/campaign**, or $0.05/email for Premium venues. Premium is a sliding-scale subscription by venue size with a 3-month minimum (annual saves 10%). A separate "Bandsintown Pro for Promoters" also exists. — [Bandsintown venues help (2024-03-12)](https://help.venues.bandsintown.com/en/articles/8487058-how-much-does-it-cost); [Pro for Promoters](https://help.promoters.bandsintown.com/en/collections/9473259-bandsintown-pro-for-promoters)

### Inferences
- The booking/settlement layer is cheap (free to ~$7.5k/yr), but it is the **control point for ticketing**. Opendate and VenuePilot give booking away to win ticketing, and Prism gets subsidized by ticketing partners. An OSS booking/settlement tool that is ticketing-neutral could slot in where Prism does, but a free one would undercut a business model that ticketing currently cross-subsidizes.
- Offers, holds, challenges and settlement sheets are standard objects across Prism, Opendate and VenuePilot. That makes them a good target for an open schema: offer → hold → confirm → contract → advance → settlement.

### Gaps
- No published prices for Opendate booking-only, Prism tiers beyond the $7,500 example, Master Tour 2026, or Elevent.
- Vendini status was not verified. My background understanding is that AudienceView acquired it around 2018, but I found no source this session.
- "Eventbrite venue tools" were not researched.
- Typical customer size per tool was not quantified beyond Opendate's 200+ operators and Prism's offer volume.

## 3. Artist-side tools (EPKs, booking platforms) and how indie artists actually get booked

### Takeaway
Artist tools are cheap subscriptions ($0–$20/mo): Bandzoogle, ReverbNation, Indie on the Move, Bandsintown for Artists (free), and Songkick (free). Actual small-venue booking in 2026 is still **direct email/DM to a named talent buyer**, with proof of local draw. Third-party "pay-to-play" bookers requiring ticket sales persist and are widely warned against.

### Cited Findings
- **Indie on the Move:** free tier (profile, messaging, availability). Premium is **$13.99/mo or $119.90/yr**, and a Deluxe tier exists (price not captured). — [IOTM pricing](https://www.indieonthemove.com/pricing)
- **ReverbNation (official):** Free $0. Basic is $12.95/mo monthly or $129.50/yr. Premium is $19.95/mo monthly or $199.50/yr. Distribution starts at $1 and "Promote It" at $21. — [ReverbNation pricing](https://reverbnation.com/pricing)
- **Bandzoogle (websites, EPK, merch):** no free plan. Lite is $6.37–$9.16/mo (sources conflict), Standard $14.16/mo, and Pro $18.33/mo, billed annually. Separate EPK and Merch Table plans exist. — [Itechguides (rechecked Sep 2026)](https://www.itechguides.com/products/bandzoogle/); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/232724/Bandzoogle/pricing)
- **Bandsintown for Artists** "is free for everyone to use". **Songkick** is free for artists per a secondary, undated source. — [Bandsintown help](https://help.artists.bandsintown.com/en/articles/7039361-does-bandsintown-for-artists-cost-money-to-use); [Music Gateway Songkick review](https://www.musicgateway.com/blog/live-music/songkick-review)
- **Sonicbids:** its ArtistData tour-date syndication service shut down in Oct 2016 and deleted profiles and data. Long-running criticism treats it as pay-to-submit. No evidence was found that Sonicbids itself formally shut down. — [Hypebot 2016](https://www.hypebot.com/hypebot/2016/09/sonicbids-to-shut-down-artistdata-to-shut-down-next-month.html); [Vice: Is Sonicbids problematic](https://www.vice.com/en/article/6edga7/is-sonicbids-problematic)
- **How booking actually happens** (blogs/vendor guides; anecdotal): find the named talent buyer rather than a generic inbox, and identify who is tagged in show announcements. Small rooms are often owner-booked, while larger rooms use a talent buyer or third-party promoter. Pitch 4–6 weeks out for rooms under 200 cap with a short email and proof of draw, and pitch full bills as packages. — [Booking-Agent.io](https://booking-agent.io/blog/how-to-get-local-gigs-without-an-agent/); [Gigwell blog](https://gigwell.com/blog/how-to-book-shows); [Orphiq](https://orphiq.com/resources/book-local-shows-independent-artist)
- **Pay-to-play:** a Dallas artist warns against third-party agencies "like MyAfton … that requires bands to sell tickets to the shows they play". A venue owner/talent buyer (Russell Hobbs, The Door) says, "Bands are better off booking directly with the club." — [Dallas Observer](https://www.dallasobserver.com/?p=40525456)
- Rising artist fees/guarantees are the #2 bottom-line pressure for indie venues (14%), after ticket sales/attendance (23%). — [Hypebot: NIVA/Bandsintown Live Pulse (2026-06-17)](https://www.hypebot.com/niva-bandsintown-survey-tracks-state-of-indie-live-events/)

### Inferences
- There is no dominant artist→venue booking marketplace for small rooms. Booking is fragmented across email, Instagram DM, IOTM's venue database, and personal relationships. The "EPK" is usually a Bandzoogle/Linktree/Google Drive link, not a structured object, so a portable, structured EPK + availability feed is an open gap.

### Gaps
- No quantitative 2026 data on the share of small-venue bookings via email vs DM vs agent vs platform.
- No data on the prevalence of presale-ticket-quota deals ("sell 20 tickets to play").
- Linktree, Gigmor, and current Sonicbids pricing were not researched. No Reddit threads were retrieved (search surfaced none), so practitioner sentiment here is thin.

## 4. Artist–venue communications: advance sheets, riders, stage plots, settlement, 1099s

### Takeaway
Advancing still runs over email + PDF/Google Docs plus proprietary tools: Master Tour on the artist side, and the advance modules in Prism and VenuePilot on the venue side. Commercial stage-plot apps (Stagediver, LiveTechPack, Stage Plot Maker, ProRider) export PDF/DOCX/CSV/PNG. **No open standard schema for stage plots, input lists, tech riders or advance sheets was found.**

### Cited Findings
- **Stagediver:** a proprietary web app for stage plots, input lists and riders with PDF export. Subscription is $9–$20/mo, with a limited free tier. — [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/stagediver/about/)
- **LiveTechPack:** exports a PDF tech pack, one-page stage plot, DOCX rider, CSV input list, and PNG. Its JSON export/import is for app backup, not a published standard. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/be/app/livetechpack-stage-plot/id6780525066)
- **ProRider Stage Designer:** shared links for co-editing, with a free tier producing watermarked PDFs. **Stage Plot Maker:** iPad/iPhone, print or email. — [ProRider](https://marlvel.ai/apps/prorider-stage-designer); [Stage Plot Maker](https://mwm.ai/apps/id/903691661)
- Prism has an "Advance tool" plus email/text templates. VenuePilot advertises "automated advancing tools". Master Tour covers day sheets, venue details and guest lists for the touring party. — [Prism](https://prism.fm/opendate/); [VenuePilot](https://venuepilot.com/pricing); [Eventric](https://eventric.rockpaperscissors.biz/artist/9906/press_clips/20124/white)
- Prism tracks "deposits, settlements, and withholding payments" on the venue side. — [Prism](https://prism.fm/opendate/)

### Inferences
- An open JSON schema for an input list, stage plot (positions + inputs + power) and hospitality/advance sheet is a **clear white space**. CSV input lists are the only de facto interchange today. Venue-side tools (Prism, VenuePilot) and artist-side tools (Master Tour, stage-plot apps) don't share a format, so advancing is re-keyed by hand.

### Gaps
- No source found on how venues handle 1099-NEC issuance for performers, or which settlement tools generate 1099s or collect W-9s. This needs targeted research (Prism "withholding" may relate to state nonresident withholding, but that is unconfirmed).
- No search was done for community or OSS stage-plot formats on GitHub (e.g. JSON specs). The absence is based on the commercial-app survey only.

## 5. Show promotion and discovery

### Takeaway
Small-venue promotion in 2026 runs mainly through Instagram (Reels/Stories) and Meta paid targeting, cross-promotion by the acts on the bill, venue email/SMS lists (Hive, Opendate, Eventbrite Pro), and Bandsintown's artist-tracker network (paid Promoted Email for venues). Local calendar listings appear to be decaying, and only anecdotal evidence was found. The Bandsintown API is **gated, read-only, and artist-scoped**, which makes it unsuitable as an open discovery backbone.

### Cited Findings
- **Bandsintown API:** access requires accepting its T&Cs plus **written consent** from Bandsintown Inc. and an app ID. It is intended for artists and their representatives, with each API key linked to a single artist unless authorized. Data "is not meant to be used in any other circumstances unless specifically approved". It is read-only, with artist info and artist events (venue embedded) endpoints. — [Bandsintown help: API](https://help.artists.bandsintown.com/en/articles/7053475-what-is-the-bandsintown-api); [Bandsintown artist ToS](https://corp.bandsintown.com/terms-artists); [Jentic catalog](https://jentic.com/apis/bandsintown.com/bandsintown)
- Bandsintown venue Promoted Email costs at least $150/campaign (see Q2). — [Bandsintown venues help](https://help.venues.bandsintown.com/en/articles/8487058-how-much-does-it-cost)
- Promotion practice (vendor guides): announce 4–6 weeks out and push hard in the final week. Other acts on the bill are "your biggest lever". Use Meta/Instagram lookalike and retargeting audiences of past attendees. Reels are Instagram's main discovery surface. — [Booking-Agent.io (2026)](https://booking-agent.io/blog/how-to-promote-your-own-show/); [Prism: digital ad primer](https://prism.fm/blog/best-practices/how-to-promote-a-concert-a-digital-advertising-primer/); [Moshville 2025](https://moshville.co.uk/misc/2025/10/how-to-promote-your-music-event-on-instagram-a-complete-guide-for-2025/)
- Local listings decay (anecdotal, New Orleans): OffBeat says many venues no longer send calendar listings, believing their own site/social is enough, and that much changed after COVID. — [OffBeat](https://offbeat.com/how-do-you-find-live-local-music)
- The NIVA/Bandsintown Live Pulse (first edition June 1–6, 2026, 212+ respondents) found **56% report advance sales down YoY**, with 20% reporting much weaker walk-up. Only 4% cite marketing/awareness as their top pressure. Average 6-month optimism is 6.8/10. — [Hypebot 2026-06-17](https://www.hypebot.com/niva-bandsintown-survey-tracks-state-of-indie-live-events/)
- NIVA baseline: 64% of independent venues operated at a loss in 2024 (2025 State of Live). — [RouteNote on NIVA State of Live](https://routenote.com/blog/nivas-state-of-live-report-shows-independent-venues-are-essential-but-under-threat/); [Axios Cleveland](https://www.axios.com/local/cleveland/2025/10/16/indie-music-venues-profitability-niva-2025)
- Opendate pitches tying the "fan record" to the show via integrated ticketing plus email/SMS. Hive integrates with VenuePilot for attendee syncing. — [Prism/Opendate page](https://prism.fm/opendate/); [Hive](https://hive.co/blog/hive-partners-with-venuepilot-to-empower-independent-venues)

### Inferences
- Discovery is the weakest link for a decentralized alternative. The aggregators (Bandsintown, Songkick, DICE's app, RA) are closed and their APIs restrictive, while local calendars are fading. An open, federated event feed that venues publish once (iCal/ActivityPub/schema.org Event) and anyone can aggregate addresses a real gap. Advance-sales softness (56% down) makes discovery economically urgent for venues.

### Gaps
- No 2026 data on Facebook Events usage decline. Resident Advisor, Do512/Do314-style calendars, and Songkick's current ownership/status (Warner) were not researched this pass. Hypebot surfaced a "Songkick to shutter" item, which refers to Songkick's 2017 ticketing arm, not the discovery app.
- Bandsintown full data-use/redistribution clauses were not read.

## 6. Payments, payouts, chargebacks, cancellation insurance

### Takeaway
Payout timing is the sharpest trust issue for small promoters. Contract platforms often hold funds until after the event (refund/chargeback exposure), while Stripe-native tools (VenuePilot) pay daily. Brown Paper Tickets' multi-year payout failures are the cautionary tale. Cancellation "insurance" is consumer-side (personal reasons). Organizer-side cancellation cover exists (Protect Group) but is niche.

### Cited Findings
- VenuePilot runs through the venue's own Stripe account and offers daily payouts. — [VenuePilot](https://venuepilot.com/pricing)
- TickPick pays the Wednesday after the event (weekly "Lightning" option). Ticket Tailor pays "fast, via its own processor" (i.e., the organizer's Stripe/PayPal/Square). — [TickPick guide](https://www.tickpick.com/blog/organizer/best-ticketing-platform-small-venues)
- A settlement vendor (Coinflow, interested party) says **2–6 weeks is normal** for settlement and that indie promoters on smaller platforms sometimes wait longer, because platforms are exposed to refunds and post-event chargebacks. — [Coinflow](https://coinflow.cash/blog/event-settlement-timeline-venues/)
- **Brown Paper Tickets:** withheld payouts to Madison venues and promoters "for months and, in some cases, for years". Phone support was offline. It still owed ~$2M of a $9M state settlement as of Oct 2022. — [Tone Madison](https://tonemadison.com/articles/complaints-about-brown-paper-tickets-continue-to-roll-in/); [BroadwayWorld 2023](https://china.broadwayworld.com/article/Events-Producers-Across-Country-Still-Owed-Thousands-Of-Dollars-By-Brown-Paper-Tickets-20231002)
- Ticketmaster Event Ticket Protector covers personal reasons, not event cancellation. Event cancellation refunds come from platform policy or law. Protect Group "Event Protect" covers organizers/platforms/venues for gross booking loss from cancellation outside their control. — [Yorkshire Post](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/business/consumer/oasis-tickets-ticketmaster-refund-policy-can-i-get-insurance-reunion-cancelled-4759189); [idloom on Event Protect](https://www.idloom.com/en/events/blog/event-insurance-idloom)
- Insurance is cited by 7% of NIVA Live Pulse respondents as their top bottom-line pressure. — [Hypebot](https://www.hypebot.com/niva-bandsintown-survey-tracks-state-of-indie-live-events/)

### Inferences
- In a decentralized design where the venue is merchant of record on its own Stripe/Square account, the venue gets instant payouts but **absorbs chargeback and refund risk directly**. That risk is what intermediaries currently price into holds. Tooling for refund workflows, dispute evidence (scan logs as proof of attendance), and optional cancellation-cover integration is a design requirement, not a nice-to-have.

### Gaps
- No primary data on Square usage by venues, chargeback rates for live events, or Eventbrite/DICE payout hold policies (no Reddit threads were surfaced). Stripe Connect platform terms for ticketing (reserve requirements for event merchants) were not researched.

## 7. Open-source ticketing/venue software and US music-venue adoption

### Takeaway
Mature OSS ticketing exists. **pretix** is the strongest: box office, check-in, and hosted at 2.5% capped €15/ticket. Others include alf.io (conference-oriented), Hi.Events, and Attendize. **No evidence was found of adoption by US music venues.** Positioning, docs, and cases are European/conference-oriented, and no OSS booking/settlement tool for venues turned up.

### Cited Findings
- **pretix.cloud (hosted):** a 2.5% system fee on the ticket price excluding tax, **max €15/ticket**. Free tickets are free up to 2,500/yr. Processing is separate (Stripe, PayPal, bank). Self-hosting has no per-ticket fee, but the Enterprise plugins (seating, lead scanning) need a license from ~€499/yr. FitGap lists €950/yr and a different free-ticket quota, so sources conflict. — [pretix.cloud pricing](https://pretix.cloud/about/en/pricing); [Chobble vs pretix](https://tickets.chobble.com/compared-to/pretix/); [FitGap pretix](https://us.fitgap.com/products/pretix)
- pretix markets to conferences, festivals, concerts, exhibitions, museums, and venue box offices, with on-site sales and check-in. A competing OSS vendor says it "has a steeper learning curve". — [Elestio pretix](https://elest.io/open-source/pretix/resources); [Hi.Events top 5 OSS ticketing (competitor)](https://blog.hi.events/top-5-open-source-event-ticketing-platforms/)
- alf.io is positioned around conferences, workshops, meetups and trade shows, with GDPR / attendee-data-control emphasis and no music mention. — [Hi.Events](https://blog.hi.events/top-5-open-source-event-ticketing-platforms/)
- Searches for music-venue adoption of pretix or alf.io returned only directory listings and competitor comparisons, no venue case studies. — [selfhostyourself: Eventbrite alternatives](https://selfhostyourself.com/alternative-to/eventbrite); [openalternative](https://openalternative.co/pretix)

### Inferences
- US music-venue adoption of OSS ticketing is likely near zero (confirmed only as absence of evidence). The barriers look operational, not technical: no US-focused hosted offering with music-venue workflows (holds, offers, settlement, door/walk-up, artist splits), no integration with Prism-style booking, and no answer for advances/working capital or discovery. pretix is a plausible ticketing engine to build on or interoperate with rather than reimplement, since it is AGPL with a plugin architecture. Its license and plugin model should be verified.

### Gaps
- No adoption data (GitHub stars, install counts, named US venues) was gathered for pretix, alf.io, Attendize, Hi.Events, Odoo Events, or Indico. Indico is CERN conference software and almost certainly irrelevant to venues, but that was not researched.
- No OSS booking/settlement/advancing project for venues was found. A targeted GitHub search is needed to confirm absence.
