# Educator survey: concert ticketing, cash and attendance

Instrument for probe 1 in `docs/PROBE_PLAN.md`. Audience: K-12 and college music educators and community ensemble directors in the US. Target n ≥ 40.

## Intro text (shown to respondents)

This short survey asks how school and community concerts are ticketed and how money and attendance are handled at the door. It takes about 5 minutes and is anonymous. Your answers will inform a free, open-source tool for school and community concerts, and we will share the summary results back with everyone who took part.

## Questions

**1. Which best describes your role?** (Single choice)
- K-12 music teacher or director, public school
- K-12 music teacher or director, private, charter or faith-based school
- College or university music faculty or staff
- Director or manager of a community band, orchestra or choir
- Music director for a church or faith-based concert series
- Parent or booster volunteer who helps run concerts
- Other (please describe)

**2. Your events in a typical year.**
- (a) About how many public performances do you organize or help run? (Number)
- (b) What is typical attendance at one of them? (Single choice: Under 100 / 100-249 / 250-499 / 500-999 / 1,000 or more)

**3. Admission and price.**
- (a) How is admission handled at your events? Select all that apply. (Multi-select: Free, no ticket or RSVP / Free, with RSVP or free reserved tickets / Free, with a suggested donation or free-will offering / Paid tickets sold in advance / Paid tickets sold at the door / Paid livestream access / Other)
- (b) If you sell tickets, what is the typical adult ticket price? (Single choice: I don't sell tickets / Under $5 / $5-$9.99 / $10-$14.99 / $15-$24.99 / $25 or more)

**4. Which of these have you used for your events in the past 12 months? Select all that apply.** (Multi-select)
- GoFan
- Hometown Ticketing
- Ludus
- On the Stage
- BookTix
- Eventbrite
- Google Forms or a similar online form
- Paper tickets or a cash box
- None (no tickets or RSVPs)
- Other (please name)

**5. Choosing a tool.**
- (a) Who chose the main tool you use now? (Single choice: The district requires it / My school or institution's administration chose it / I chose it / A booster club or parent group chose it / Our ensemble's board or committee chose it / We don't use a tool / Don't know)
- (b) If you wanted to use a different tool next season, which best describes what it would take? (Single choice: I could decide myself / I would need approval from my principal, chair or board / The district would need to approve it, for example through a contract or data privacy agreement / The district requires one specific vendor, so I could not switch / Don't know)

**6. Fees.**
- (a) On a typical ticket, about how much do buyers pay in fees on top of the ticket price? (Single choice: No fees / Less than $1 / $1.00-$1.99 / $2.00-$2.99 / $3.00 or more / Don't know / Not applicable)
- (b) Does your program pay any of these? Select all that apply. (Multi-select: Per-ticket fees / Card processing fees / An annual, subscription or contract fee / Fees on cash or complimentary tickets / None / Don't know)
- (c) How much of a concern are ticketing fees, for your families or your program? (Likert 1-5: 1 = Not a concern, 5 = A major concern; plus Not applicable)

**7. Cash handling.**
- (a) For a typical event, about how many hours do you and your volunteers spend on cash: setting up a cash box, counting, reconciling and depositing? Enter 0 if you don't handle cash. (Number, hours)
- (b) Which rules apply to cash at your events? Select all that apply. (Multi-select: Deposit within a set time, such as 24 hours / Two people must count / Receipt book or numbered tickets required / Money must go into a school or district account / A booster club handles the money / No written rules / Don't know / Not applicable)
- (c) How burdensome is cash handling and reconciliation for you? (Likert 1-5: 1 = Not at all burdensome, 5 = Very burdensome; plus Not applicable)

**8. Free events and headcount.**
- (a) Which of these apply to your free events? Select all that apply. (Multi-select: We have plenty of room and don't track attendance / We take RSVPs or issue free reserved tickets / We estimate from past years / We have run out of seats at least once in the past two years / Many people who RSVP don't come / We don't hold free events)
- (b) How much of a problem is knowing or controlling attendance at free events? (Likert 1-5: 1 = Not a problem, 5 = A major problem; plus Not applicable)

**9. Programs and donations.**
- (a) What kind of programs do you provide? (Single choice: Printed only / Digital only, for example a QR code or link / Both printed and digital / No programs)
- (b) How do you collect donations, if at all? Select all that apply. (Multi-select: We don't collect donations / Cash or free-will offering at the door / Online donation link / Text-to-give or QR code to give / Donation added at ticket checkout / A booster club or separate fundraiser handles it / Other)

**10. Which accessibility requirements have you been asked to meet for your events or ticketing? Select all that apply.** (Multi-select)
- Reservable wheelchair and companion seating
- Accessible website or ticketing pages (for example, WCAG or ADA web rules)
- An accessibility statement or VPAT from the ticketing vendor
- Large-print or screen-reader-friendly programs
- Assistive listening devices
- Sign language interpretation
- None that I know of
- Don't know
- Other (please describe)

**11. Your current setup.**
- (a) Overall, how satisfied are you with how your events are ticketed and managed now? (Likert 1-5: 1 = Very dissatisfied, 5 = Very satisfied)
- (b) How likely are you to consider a different tool or method in the next 12 months? (Likert 1-5: 1 = Very unlikely, 5 = Very likely)

**12. Which of these frustrate you most? Rank up to three; leave the rest blank.** (Ranking, top 3)
- Fees charged to families
- Fees or costs to my program
- Handling, counting and depositing cash
- Planning attendance for free events
- Complimentary tickets for performers' families
- Producing programs
- Collecting donations
- Reports for my school, district or treasurer
- Knowing who attended or contacting my audience
- The vendor marketing to my families
- Meeting accessibility requirements
- Getting approval to use a tool
- Nothing; the current setup works for me

**13. If a free tool were available, which features would matter most to you? Select up to five.** (Multi-select, maximum 5)
- No fees for families
- No fees for my program
- Free RSVP with a seat limit
- Complimentary tickets for performers' families
- Door check-in that works without Wi-Fi
- Card and cash sales at the door
- A cash count and deposit report for my school or treasurer
- Digital programs
- Donations or free-will offerings
- An audience email list my program owns
- Works without an app (print-at-home ticket or name at the door)
- Livestream access
- Pages that meet accessibility requirements
- Other (please describe)
- None; I would stay with my current setup

**14. Would you be willing to try a free tool for one event this school year and give feedback?** (Single choice: Yes / Maybe, tell me more / No)
- Optional: if yes or maybe, leave an email address. (Short text)

Implementation note: collect the email through a separate linked form so the survey answers stay anonymous and cannot be joined to an address.

**15. Anything else we should know about how your events are ticketed, staffed or funded?** (Short text, open-ended)

## Scoring and decision rule

### Valid responses

Count a response as valid if Q1 is answered with anything other than an "Other" that describes someone who does not organize events, and Q2(a) is 1 or more. Remove obvious duplicates (identical answers submitted within minutes). The target is **n ≥ 40 valid responses**, with a stretch goal of 80 and at least 20 K-12 respondents (Q1 options 1 and 2) so assumption 2 has a usable subgroup.

### Derived measures

| Measure | Definition |
|---|---|
| Fee pain | Q6(c) = 4 or 5 |
| Cash pain | Q7(c) = 4 or 5, counted among respondents who handle cash (Q7(a) > 0, or Q4 includes "Paper tickets or a cash box") |
| RSVP pain | Q8(b) = 4 or 5, counted among respondents who hold free events |
| Pain-positive | Any one of fee pain, cash pain or RSVP pain |
| Switch-open | Q11(b) = 4 or 5, or Q14 = Yes or Maybe |
| Satisfied, no pain | Q11(a) = 4 or 5 and none of Q6(c), Q7(c), Q8(b) at 4 or above |
| Free-to-school vendor user | Q4 includes GoFan, Hometown Ticketing, Ludus or On the Stage |
| District-locked | Q5(a) = "The district requires it" or Q5(b) = "The district requires one specific vendor" |
| District approval needed | Q5(b) = "The district would need to approve it" |

Report "Don't know" and "Not applicable" counts but exclude them from the denominator of the item they apply to. If more than 30% of K-12 respondents answer "Don't know" on Q5(b), that is itself a finding: teachers do not know their procurement rules, and the probe 2 calls decide assumption 2.

### Assumption 1: educators feel fee, cash and RSVP pain strongly enough to switch

Report's falsifier: most report "district handles it" or satisfaction with free-to-school vendors.

| Verdict | Rule (all percentages of valid responses) |
|---|---|
| **Pass** | At least 50% pain-positive, AND at least 25% both pain-positive and switch-open, AND at least 5 respondents answer Yes to Q14 with a contact email |
| **Fail** | Fewer than 35% pain-positive, OR more than 50% "satisfied, no pain", OR fewer than 3 Yes answers to Q14 |
| **Inconclusive** | Anything between. Extend the survey to n ≥ 80 through more channels before deciding, or decide on subgroups as below |

Additional checks, reported but not part of the verdict:
- Among free-to-school vendor users, the share who are "satisfied, no pain." If it is above 60%, the zero-fee pitch is weak against those vendors even if the overall result passes.
- Which pain leads. If cash or RSVP pain is common but fee pain is not, v1 should lead with door reconciliation and free RSVP rather than zero fees.
- Q12 rank counts (3 points for first, 2 for second, 1 for third) and Q13 selection counts, to order the v1 feature list. These are for scoping, not for the pass or fail decision.
- Q10 counts, to confirm whether accessibility documentation (VPAT, WCAG) is a procurement gate that v1 must meet.

### Assumption 2: fine-arts ticketing is not locked to district-mandated vendors

Computed among K-12 respondents only (Q1 options 1 and 2). Report's falsifier: a majority must use the athletics vendor or need a signed DPA from a legal entity.

| Verdict | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Pass (survey side)** | At most 33% district-locked, AND at most 50% district-locked or district-approval-needed combined |
| **Fail (survey side)** | More than 50% district-locked, OR more than 50% district-approval-needed (because district approval usually means a contract or DPA that needs a legal entity to sign) |
| **Inconclusive** | Anything between |

The survey measures what teachers believe; the five coordinator calls in probe 2 measure policy. If the two disagree, probe 2 governs the policy question, and K-12 is treated as failed until a coordinator confirms that a teacher can use a free tool without a district contract.

### Subgroups

Compute assumption 1 separately for K-12, college, and community and church respondents when a subgroup has at least 10 valid responses. Report smaller subgroups but do not decide on them. A pass among community, church or college respondents combined with a K-12 fail on either assumption supports the "Proceed, narrowed" outcome in `docs/PROBE_PLAN.md`.

## Distribution plan

The research found no existing survey of music educators' ticketing practices, so this instrument fills a real gap. Offering the aggregate results back to respondents and channel owners is both the incentive and a likely conference talk.

| Channel | How | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Ben's conference sessions | QR code on the last slide; allow 3 minutes at the end of the session | If no session falls in weeks 1-3, rely on the lists below; later sessions add responses but the week-4 gate uses what is in by day 21 |
| State MEA (music educators association) email lists | Ask the executive director or newsletter editor to include a short paragraph and link | Start with states where Ben has contacts; one tagged link per state |
| State MEA and music-teacher Facebook groups | Ask the group admin before posting; follow group rules | Post once, then one reminder a week later |
| NAfME community forums | Post in the relevant community with the plain-language intro | Neutral wording; no product pitch |
| College music department listservs | Ask a faculty contact or department chair to forward | Reaches recital and ensemble coordinators |
| Community band association lists | Ask the association (for example, the Association of Concert Bands) to share in a newsletter or member email | Also reaches community orchestras and choirs through member referrals |

Practical rules:
- Use one tagged link per channel (a hidden field or separate form copy) so sources can be compared without asking for location.
- Open in week 1, send one reminder in week 2, close at the end of day 21, analyze in week 4.
- Keep every post neutral: describe the survey, not the tool. Do not show product mockups or claim savings.
- The survey is for adults who organize or help run events; student respondents are out of scope.
